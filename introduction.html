Introduction

The Practitioner-Researcher Chasm

The field of software engineering is characterized by a fundamental dichotomy in the creation and dissemination of knowledge.

Academic Literature

Formal, rigorously peer-reviewed literature produced by researchers and published in journals and conference proceedings, controlled by academic publishers.

Grey Literature

Vast and dynamic body of work produced by practitioners, software engineers, architects, and technical leaders within the industry ecosystem.

Structural Barriers

For Practitioners:

  • Academic journals behind expensive paywalls
  • Dense and theoretical prose
  • Time required to parse scientific papers

For Researchers:

  • View GL as non-scientific or anecdotal
  • Lack of formal independent peer-review process

Result: Chasm between state-of-the-art (academic) and state-of-the-practice (industry)

Grey Literature Proliferation

Direct functional response to structural gap. Practitioners need:

  • Current information
  • Practical information
  • Context-specific information
  • Solutions to immediate real-world problems

Distribution channels include blogs, forums, and technical reports, characterized as agile, accessible, and aligned with practitioner needs. This represents a necessary purpose-built infrastructure for knowledge exchange.

Formal Definition of Grey Literature

Luxembourg Definition (1997, expanded 2004)

"That which is produced on all levels of government, academics, business and industry in print and electronic formats, but which is not controlled by commercial publishers"

Key Criterion: Lack of control by commercial publishing entities

Software Engineering Context

Functional Definitions:

  • Any literature not subjected to formal independent peer-review process before publication
  • Material not indexed by major academic database vendors (ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore)
